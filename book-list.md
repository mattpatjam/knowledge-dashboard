# Knowledge Dashboard — Curated Book List

Curated list for sourcing — titles/authors only, no files included. Books to be added to Drive by Matthieu directly.

## 1. Trading

- **Reminiscences of a Stock Operator** by Edwin Lefèvre — The century-old classic on speculation psychology, still the most recommended trading book ever written.
- **Market Wizards** by Jack D. Schwager — The definitive interview series with the world's top traders, the standard reference on how elite traders actually think.
- **Technical Analysis of the Financial Markets** by John J. Murphy — The most widely used technical analysis textbook among retail traders and CMT candidates, a solid reference but not how institutional desks actually trade.
- **Trading in the Zone** by Mark Douglas — The canonical book on trading psychology and discipline, cited by nearly every professional trading curriculum.
- **How to Make Money in Stocks** by William J. O'Neil — Origin of the CANSLIM system from the founder of Investor's Business Daily, a decades-long growth-trading standard.
- **Option Volatility and Pricing** by Sheldon Natenberg — The standard reference on options and derivatives, closest thing market-making desks have to a bible, fills the gap this list had beyond chart-reading.
- **The New Trading for a Living** by Alexander Elder — The most widely used all-in-one manual covering psychology, risk management, and trade mechanics.
- **Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom** by Van K. Tharp — The reference work on position sizing and system design, foundational in professional risk management.

## 2. Personal Finance & Investing

- **The Intelligent Investor** by Benjamin Graham — Warren Buffett calls it the best book on investing ever written; the foundation of value investing since 1949.
- **The Little Book of Common Sense Investing** by John C. Bogle — The index-fund manifesto from Vanguard's founder, the man who invented the product. Kept as the one buy-and-hold-index pick; dropped the two other books making the same argument.
- **The Psychology of Money** by Morgan Housel — The modern standard on money behavior, a multi-million-copy bestseller adopted across the finance industry within a few years.
- **One Up On Wall Street** by Peter Lynch — The most famous fund manager of his era explaining how individual investors can win; a permanent fixture on best-of lists.
- **The Millionaire Next Door** by Thomas J. Stanley & William D. Danko — The landmark research study on how real wealth is actually built, cited for nearly 30 years.
- **Your Money or Your Life** by Vicki Robin & Joe Dominguez — The founding text of the financial-independence movement, in print since 1992.
- **The Most Important Thing** by Howard Marks — Oaktree's founder on risk, cycles, and second-level thinking, the professional-grade complement to Graham's classic.

## 3. Real Estate

- **The Millionaire Real Estate Investor** by Gary Keller — The standard framework book for building a rental portfolio. Kept over Keller's companion "Agent" title, which is a sales-recruitment manual, not an asset book.
- **What Every Real Estate Investor Needs to Know About Cash Flow** by Frank Gallinelli — The reference text on deal math (cap rate, NOI, IRR), used in university courses.
- **The Book on Rental Property Investing** by Brandon Turner — The BiggerPockets-era standard for buy-and-hold investing, the most gifted modern rental book.
- **The ABCs of Real Estate Investing** by Ken McElroy — The widely cited practitioner primer on finding, evaluating, and operating multifamily property.
- **Real Estate Development: Principles and Process** by Mike E. Miles, Laurence M. Netherton & Adrienne Schmitz — The Urban Land Institute textbook, the academic standard on development.
- **Ninja Selling** by Larry Kendall — The most respected modern book on relationship-based real estate sales, standard training material in top brokerages.
- **The Real Estate Game** by William Poorvu — The Harvard Business School standard on commercial development and deal structuring, the institutional-grade read this category was missing.

## 4. Health

- **Outlive** by Peter Attia — The modern reference on longevity medicine, a #1 bestseller that reset how the mainstream talks about healthspan.
- **Why We Sleep** by Matthew Walker — The most influential general-audience book on sleep science, though several central claims have been publicly disputed for data and citation errors. Read it for the case on sleep hygiene, not as a citation source.
- **Exercised** by Daniel E. Lieberman — Harvard evolutionary biologist's evidence-based account of physical activity, widely cited against fitness myths.
- **In Defense of Food** by Michael Pollan — The most durable general-audience book on sane eating ("Eat food. Not too much. Mostly plants.").
- **The Blue Zones** by Dan Buettner — The landmark longevity field study of the world's longest-lived populations, though the underlying demographic data has been challenged (pension-fraud and bad birth records in some regions). Treat the lifestyle takeaways as directional, not proven.
- **Spark** by John J. Ratey — The standard reference on exercise and the brain, from a Harvard psychiatry professor.
- **Breath** by James Nestor — The modern bestseller that brought breathing science to a mass audience, grounded in published research.
- **Starting Strength** by Mark Rippetoe — The canonical barbell training manual, the default technical reference for lifting fundamentals.

## 5. Customer Support

- **The Effortless Experience** by Matthew Dixon, Nick Toman & Rick DeLisi — The research-backed standard on support operations; effort reduction beats delight.
- **Setting the Table** by Danny Meyer — The hospitality bible on "enlightened hospitality," read far beyond the restaurant industry. Kept over Nordstrom and Disney's service books, which cover the same "high-touch service" ground with less depth.
- **Uncommon Service** by Frances Frei & Anne Morriss — The Harvard Business School treatment of service as strategy, not a department.
- **Hug Your Haters** by Jay Baer — The modern standard on handling complaints and public feedback in the social media era.
- **Customer Success** by Nick Mehta, Dan Steinman & Lincoln Murphy — The standard reference on retention and churn prevention for recurring-revenue and B2B businesses, the modern counterpart to the hospitality-era service classics this category leaned on.

## 6. Marketing

- **Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind** by Al Ries & Jack Trout — The book that defined positioning; the single most cited marketing strategy text.
- **Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion** by Robert B. Cialdini — The scientific foundation of persuasion (the 7 principles), required reading across marketing and sales.
- **Ogilvy on Advertising** by David Ogilvy — The advertising classic from the industry's most famous practitioner, still standard agency reading.
- **Breakthrough Advertising** by Eugene M. Schwartz — The copywriter's copywriting book; the origin of "stages of awareness," legendary in direct response. Kept over Claude Hopkins' "Scientific Advertising," which covers the same ground but reads as a historical relic now.
- **This Is Marketing** by Seth Godin — Godin's fuller, more actionable treatment of standing out, replaces the thinner "Purple Cow."
- **Building a StoryBrand** by Donald Miller — The current standard framework for message clarity, adopted by thousands of companies as their messaging system.
- **$100M Offers** by Alex Hormozi — The modern standard on offer construction, already canonical in direct-response and agency circles.
- **Contagious: Why Things Catch On** by Jonah Berger — The Wharton research standard on why content and products spread.
- **How Brands Grow** by Byron Sharp — The most important empirical marketing book of the past 20 years, uses real purchasing data to challenge classical positioning theory head-on. Deliberately paired with "Positioning" for the tension.
- **$100M Leads** by Alex Hormozi — Hormozi's lead-generation and attention companion to $100M Offers, standard modern reference on getting strangers to want to buy.
- **$100M Money Models** by Alex Hormozi — Hormozi's follow-up on monetization and offer sequencing, the natural pair to his other two titles already on this list.

## 7. HR

- **Who: The A Method for Hiring** by Geoff Smart & Randy Street — The most recommended hiring book in business; the structured interview standard. Kept over "Topgrading" (Geoff's father Bradford's book), which is the same method built for enterprise bureaucracy.
- **First, Break All the Rules** by Marcus Buckingham & Curt Coffman — Gallup's landmark study of great managers, foundation of the engagement (Q12) movement.
- **Work Rules!** by Laszlo Bock — Google's former head of People Operations on data-driven HR; the modern people-ops reference.
- **Powerful** by Patty McCord — The Netflix culture architect's book, source of the most influential culture philosophy of the last two decades.
- **Drive** by Daniel H. Pink — The standard general-audience synthesis of motivation science (autonomy, mastery, purpose).
- **The Culture Code** by Daniel Coyle — The most cited modern book on how high-performing group cultures are actually built.
- **The Alliance** by Reid Hoffman, Ben Casnocha & Chris Yeh — LinkedIn's co-founder on managing employment as a series of mutual-benefit "tours of duty," the most honest modern take on retention and loyalty this category was missing.

## 8. Coaching & Training

- **Coaching for Performance** by Sir John Whitmore — The founding text of performance coaching and the GROW model, the profession's standard reference. Kept over "The Inner Game of Tennis," the source material Whitmore adapted into a business framework.
- **The Coaching Habit** by Michael Bungay Stanier — The best-selling modern coaching book; seven questions that became standard manager training worldwide.
- **Peak: Secrets from the New Science of Expertise** by Anders Ericsson & Robert Pool — The definitive book on deliberate practice from the researcher who discovered it.
- **Make It Stick** by Peter C. Brown, Henry L. Roediger III & Mark A. McDaniel — The evidence-based standard on how learning actually works, core reading for training design.
- **Design for How People Learn** by Julie Dirksen — The modern practitioner standard for instructional and course design, more actionable than "Telling Ain't Training," which it replaces here.
- **Coaching Salespeople into Sales Champions** by Keith Rosen — The most recognized book specifically on sales coaching frameworks for managers.
- **The Great CEO Within** by Matt Mochary — A tactical operator's manual for coaching and running a company day to day, replaces "Trillion Dollar Coach," which is more tribute than instruction manual.

## 9. Negotiation & Closing

- **Never Split the Difference** by Chris Voss & Tahl Raz — The former FBI hostage negotiator's playbook, the dominant modern negotiation book (tactical empathy, calibrated questions).
- **Getting to Yes** by Roger Fisher & William Ury — The Harvard Negotiation Project classic that defined principled negotiation; the academic standard for 40+ years. Kept over Ury's own sequel, "Getting Past No," which covers the same ground.
- **Bargaining for Advantage** by G. Richard Shell — The Wharton negotiation course in book form, the most respected systematic treatment of negotiation strategy.
- **SPIN Selling** by Neil Rackham — The largest research study ever done on selling (35,000 sales calls), the foundation of consultative and question-based selling.
- **The Challenger Sale** by Matthew Dixon & Brent Adamson — The defining modern B2B sales book, built on CEB's large-scale research into top performers.
- **Pitch Anything** by Oren Klaff — The most cited book on frame control and high-stakes pitching, standard in capital-raising and big-ticket sales.
- **The Qualified Sales Leader** by John McMahon — The definitive modern book on enterprise sales qualification (MEDDPICC), replaces the dated positional-bargaining and high-pressure-closing titles this category carried before.
- **Predictable Revenue** by Aaron Ross & Marylou Tyler — The Salesforce outbound bible (Cold Calling 2.0), the standard reference on building a dedicated SDR/prospecting engine separate from closing.

## 10. Leadership & Management

- **The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People** by Stephen R. Covey — One of the best-selling leadership books ever; its vocabulary is embedded in management culture worldwide.
- **The Effective Executive** by Peter F. Drucker — The classic from the father of modern management, still the reference on executive self-management.
- **High Output Management** by Andrew S. Grove — Intel's legendary CEO on management as leverage; the operating manual of choice in tech and beyond.
- **Good to Great** by Jim Collins — The most cited business research study on what separates enduringly great companies (Level 5 Leadership, Hedgehog Concept).
- **The Five Dysfunctions of a Team** by Patrick Lencioni — The standard framework for team health, used in leadership offsites everywhere for 20+ years.
- **Turn the Ship Around!** by L. David Marquet — A submarine captain's account of pushing decision-making down the chain, a modern, decentralized counterpart to "Extreme Ownership," which it replaces here.
- **The Hard Thing About Hard Things** by Ben Horowitz — The most respected book on managing through crisis, the founder/CEO reference from a top VC.
- **Radical Candor** by Kim Scott — The current standard on feedback and people management, adopted as a management framework across industries.
- **Rework** by Jason Fried & David Heinemeier Hansson — Basecamp's founders on running lean, remote, and asynchronous, the modern counterweight to the traditional corporate-management canon this category leaned on.

## 11. Systems & Operations

- **Traction** by Gino Wickman — The standard operating system (EOS) for turning founder-led chaos into repeatable process; the most direct fit for the systemization gap this list was missing.
- **The E-Myth Revisited** by Michael Gerber — The foundational case for why being great at the work doesn't mean you're great at running the business, the classic argument for building systems instead of grinding harder.

## 12. Fundraising, Equity & Deal Structuring

- **Venture Deals** by Brad Feld & Jason Mendelson — The standard reference on term sheets, cap tables, vesting schedules and ESOPs, written for the operator side of the table rather than the investor side.
